Before you start

The signal in the static.

AI companies don't have an awareness problem. They have a noise problem. Everyone is talking about AI, and most of it is surface-level hype. Ads, cold outreach and your own posts all blend into the feed.

What cuts through is a trusted creator who knows signal from noise, speaking to an audience that can tell the difference. This playbook is how we make that happen, chapter by chapter, with a working tool in each.

Shift 01

Your buyer is technical

Developers, ML engineers and technical founders test claims before they believe them. They reward depth and punish hype within minutes.

Shift 02

Trust is borrowed

A new AI product has no reputation yet. The fastest way to earn one is to borrow it from people who spent years earning theirs.

Shift 03

Feeds beat funnels

The decision starts in creator feeds, newsletters and podcasts, long before a buyer reaches your site.

Chapter 01 · p.3

Picking creators by signal, not size.

Follower count tells you how many people might see a post. It says nothing about how many will believe it. A creator with 40,000 engineers who copy their workflows moves more pipeline than one with 600,000 followers and a new "tool of the day" every morning. Reach is the last tiebreaker, never the first filter.

Red flags we screen out

8 checks
  1. Every post is a list of "AI tools you need", with a different sponsor in each slot.
  2. A sudden follower jump with no viral post to explain it.
  3. Replies full of generic praise from accounts with no history.
  4. No hands-on content in 30 days, only commentary and reposts.
  5. Won't try the product before agreeing to post about it.
  6. Inconsistent or missing sponsorship disclosure.
  7. An audience in the wrong geography or seniority for your buyer.
  8. Rates based on follower count, with no performance history.
Field testBefore you sign anyone, ask them to explain your product back in three sentences after 20 minutes of use. If they can't, their audience won't understand it either.
Chapter 02 · p.7

The brief that earns trust.

A good brief controls the story. It never controls the voice. The moment a creator reads a script, their audience hears an advert. Every GRVTY campaign starts with a content brief built on real product understanding and competitive white space, so the creator can say something true and specific in their own words.

Eight parts of a brief that works

Anatomy
  1. The one thing. One takeaway the viewer remembers tomorrow. List three and they remember none.
  2. Product understanding. What it does, who it's for, the job it replaces. Written for a smart outsider.
  3. Competitive white space. The gap you fill that nothing else does well. This is the angle worth watching.
  4. Proof path. A real task they can finish on camera in under ten minutes, with access and a direct line to your team.
  5. Guardrails. Claims they can make, claims they must not, and benchmarks that need context.
  6. Creative freedom. Format, hook, tone, length and honest criticism are explicitly theirs.
  7. The ask. One call to action, one tracked link, one offer.
  8. Disclosure and timing. How the partnership is labelled, the posting window, review turnaround.

Working rules

Trust vs. tolerance
Always
  • Give access at least 7 days before the posting window.
  • Put a technical contact on call for creator questions.
  • Let them say what the product doesn't do well.
  • Approve for accuracy, never for tone.
  • One round of feedback, turned around in 24 hours.
Never
  • Send a script, or talking points that read like one.
  • Ask every creator for the same hook.
  • Ask them to soften or hide the disclosure.
  • Put five calls to action in one post.
  • Rewrite their caption in your brand voice.

Chapter 03 · p.11

Mapping distribution to your buyers.

Distribution follows the audience, not a channel template. A developer evaluating an API, an enterprise lead building a business case and a prosumer hunting for a shortcut decide in different places, at different speeds. Each channel does a different job. Match the job to your buyer and their stage.

Chapter 04 · p.15

Defining success before you spend.

Decide what success means after the campaign, and every campaign looks like a success. For one team it's API keys created in week one. For another it's twelve qualified enterprise demos. We agree the definition before the first brief is written, then build attribution around that one number.

Attribution that holds up

Step 3
  1. One tracked link per creator, per channel. Compare voices and formats, not just campaigns.
  2. Creator-specific offer codes. Catches buyers who type the URL instead of clicking.
  3. "Where did you hear about us?" Free text at sign-up captures what links miss, especially podcasts.
  4. A two-week baseline. Measure before launch, then report lift above it.
  5. A 30-day halo window. Technical buyers research before they act.
  6. A weekly read-out. Same format every week, so the team acts on trends.
